An autopsy of a safety behaviour.

Open weights let anyone quietly fine-tune a model's safety back out. This project detects that from the model's own activations at AUROC 0.956, then shows the method holds across a different model, an image channel, and a different hidden behaviour.

Instruction-tuned language models learn to refuse harmful requests. This project treats that refusal like a forensic subject: locate where it lives, attack it, break it with a covert fine-tune, then detect the tampering from the model's internal signals. The same recipe then runs on a second model, a second modality, and a second behaviour.

Built on Arditi et al. 2024 (refusal is one direction), replicating GCG and PAIR, and extending six more papers on backdoors, deception, and cross-modal safety (sources below).

Why it matters: an open-weight AI model can be quietly fine-tuned to strip its safety, with no obvious sign in the weights. This project builds a way to catch that from the model's internal activity, and shows it holds up under attack and across settings.

A chatbot's refusal is not spread across its whole brain. It rides on a single internal direction, like one dial. Turn the dial down and the model stops refusing. An attacker can covertly retrain a model to drop its refusal entirely. Even then, the model leaves a fingerprint in its internal activity the moment it decides whether to comply. This dashboard shows how well each attack works, where refusal lives, and how well we catch a tampered model, like a lie detector for AI safety.

01

The lifecycle

locate → attack → break → detect, on Llama-3.1-8B

In plain terms: First we measure how often the model refuses harmful prompts (it should be high) and how often it wrongly refuses safe ones (should be near zero). Then we try to jailbreak it three ways and rank them. We find the single "refusal dial" and switch it off. We secretly retrain the model to never refuse, then check whether a detector reading its internal signals can catch that tampering.

    02

    Generalization

    does the method transfer?

    In plain terms: One result could be a fluke. So we run the same recipe on a different model, on harmful requests hidden inside images, and on a different hidden behaviour (a model that deliberately plays dumb). If the method keeps working, the mechanism is real, not an accident of one setup.

      03

      Robustness

      does the method survive scrutiny?

      In plain terms: We stress-test the findings. Is refusal really one dial or a few? Is the detector a genuine handle on the behaviour or just a correlation? Can a clever attacker who knows about the detector slip past it? Can the damage be undone? The honest answers are here.

        04

        Threat breadth

        does the detector reach other covert tampers?

        In plain terms: The same detector, unchanged, is pointed at other kinds of hidden sabotage: a model quietly turned broadly harmful, a "sleeper agent" that only misbehaves on a secret trigger word, a model that lies strategically, and refusal happening inside a reasoning model's private thoughts. It catches them too.
